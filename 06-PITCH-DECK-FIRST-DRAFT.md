## SLIDE 2: THE PROBLEM

### 50M African Retailers Pay 30-40% Supply Chain Premiums

**Three Critical Problems**:

1. **Fragmented Suppliers**
   - Average retailer works with 10-15 different suppliers
   - No single source for bulk purchasing
   - Inconsistent pricing and quality
   - Time-consuming vendor management

2. **Geographic Barriers**
   - Suppliers concentrated in capital cities (Nairobi, Accra, Dakar)
   - Regional retailers pay premium for logistics
   - Import taxes + intermediaries inflate costs
   - Limited cross-border trade options

3. **No Platform for Coordination**
   - Retailers lack visibility into wholesale options
   - No standardized ordering/delivery system
   - Payment friction (cash-heavy, no mobile integration)
   - Information asymmetry favors suppliers

**By the Numbers**:
- **Market Size**: 50M+ retailers across East & West Africa
- **Supply Cost Premium**: 30-40% above wholesale
- **Annual Wasted Spend**: USD 50B+ in inefficiencies
- **Current Tools**: Spreadsheets, phone calls, cash payments

### Common Investor Questions & Answers:

**Q: "Why now? Why not sooner or later?"**
> A: Three factors converged. First, AfCFTA eliminated trade barriers—cross-border supply chains are now viable. Second, mobile money penetration hit 40% in East Africa—payments are frictionless. Third, retailers are moving online—smartphone penetration increasing. This window is NOW, but it won't stay open. In 3-5 years, a mega-platform will consolidate the market. First-mover advantage is critical.

**Q: "Why will retailers choose you vs. going to suppliers directly?"**
> A: Because retailers can't. Manufacturers won't sell in small quantities to one retailer—they need wholesale minimums (50+ units, KES 1M+). We aggregate demand from 1,000+ retailers, so manufacturers are happy to deal with us. Retailers get access they can't get individually.

**Q: "What's your competitive advantage?"**
> A: We're doing end-to-end: platform + warehouse + logistics. Competitors are doing pieces. Plus, we have founder operational credibility (12 years in supply chain), and we're building a network effect (more retailers = more suppliers incentivized = more retailers attracted). Winner-take-most market dynamics favor the first-mover.

**Q: "Why should I believe your unit economics?"**
> A: Because they're verified by customers. We have 50 retailers with 90% repeat rate and NPS 72. Average LTV is USD 8K-12K (proven by actual spend data). CAC is USD 25-50 (documented acquisition costs). The 160:1 LTV:CAC ratio is exceptional, but it's real. Happy to share detailed customer data in diligence.

**Q: "What if a competitor enters with more capital?"**
> A: Capital doesn't buy relationships or execution. Building a supply chain network requires trust—manufacturers don't switch partners easily. We've invested 6+ months building supplier relationships. Plus, capital alone won't solve logistics (you need local teams, warehouse infrastructure, execution). This isn't a software winner-take-all. It's a logistics network—the advantage goes to the first operator who builds it.
