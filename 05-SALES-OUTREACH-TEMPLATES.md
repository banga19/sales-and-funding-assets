## FOLLOW-UP SEQUENCE (After Initial Email)

### Timeline for Cold Outreach:
```
Day 1:  Initial email
Day 5:  No response → Follow-up #1 (reminder)
Day 12: No response → Follow-up #2 (value add: cost analysis)
Day 19: No response → Move to next prospect or warm intro strategy
```

### Cadence for Warm Leads:
```
Day 1:  Initial email (from warm intro)
Day 3:  If no response → LinkedIn message
Day 7:  If no response → Phone call attempt
Day 10: If no response → Email with specific offer
```

## PERSONALIZATION CHECKLIST

Before sending ANY email:

- [ ] Research company's recent projects (Google, LinkedIn, industry news)
- [ ] Identify procurement challenges (staffing changes, recent delays, new developments)
- [ ] Find 1-2 specific data points about their industry/market
- [ ] Reference a recent company announcement or growth signal
- [ ] Customize the material types to their actual focus
- [ ] Verify email address is correct (LinkedIn, company website, email finder)
- [ ] Adjust tone to match their company culture (formal vs. casual)

**Example**: 
> "I noticed [Company] just announced the new [Project] development. Managing materials across multiple sites gets complex—especially with the current supply chain delays. We specialize in exactly this challenge."

## PHONE SCRIPT (After Email, During Discovery Call)

**Opening (1 min)**:
> "Hi [Name], thanks for taking the time. I'll keep this brief—15 minutes as promised. The reason I reached out is that we help construction companies like [Company Name] reduce material procurement costs by 15-20%. Quick question first: What's your biggest procurement headache right now?"

**Listen** (2 min):
- Let them talk. Take notes on:
  - Supplier challenges
  - Volume/spend level
  - Pain points
  - Current process

**Problem-Solution (3 min)**:
> "I hear you on [their pain point]. That's exactly what we're solving. We've built a regional supply network—direct relationships with 50+ manufacturers across Kenya, Uganda, Ghana, Senegal. This means you get one point of contact, better pricing, faster delivery, and quality consistency."

**Proof (2 min)**:
> "A contractor similar to your company—also doing [relevant project type]—saved KES 2M on their first order. That was just one material category. Across your procurement, the potential is significant."

**Pilot (2 min)**:
> "Here's what I propose: Let's start small. Pick one material category where you have the highest spend—cement, steel, whatever. We pilot one order. You'll see the savings, quality, and speed. If it works, we expand to your full procurement. Sound fair?"

**Close (1 min)**:
> "Perfect. Here's what I'll do: I'll send over a 1-page proposal outlining the pilot—materials, pricing, timeline. You review, and we'll sync up next week to confirm. Cool?"

## OBJECTION HANDLING

### Objection: "We're happy with our current suppliers"

**Response**: 
> "I hear that. Most contractors we work with are also happy with their suppliers individually. The issue isn't supplier satisfaction—it's that coordinating 10-15 suppliers is expensive and inefficient. We're not replacing your suppliers; we're consolidating the best ones and reducing your overhead. Let's pilot with one category—if there's no value, you've lost 15 minutes. If there is, you save 18% on material costs. Fair?"

### Objection: "How do I know you can deliver on time?"

**Response**:
> "Great question. We guarantee on-time delivery with an SLA. If we miss, you get a credit on the order. We're also not your sole supplier—you still work with your current vendors if something falls through. But our track record across 50+ customers is 95%+ on-time delivery. We can start with a small pilot to build trust."

### Objection: "This sounds risky. We prefer established suppliers."

**Response**:
> "I get it. That's why we don't ask for a long-term commitment. Pilot first. One order. Minimal risk. You see the results, then decide if it makes sense for your company. Plus, you're not relying on us exclusively—we're supplementing your current supply chain with better pricing."

### Objection: "What's your minimum order?"

**Response**:
> "No minimums. We work with order sizes from KES 50K to KES 5M+. Flexibility is the point—we consolidate orders from multiple customers, so we can offer competitive pricing without forcing you into massive commitments."
